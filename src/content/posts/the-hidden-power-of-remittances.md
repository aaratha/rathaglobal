title: The Hidden Power of Remittances in Development Finance

description: Remittances now exceed foreign direct investment in many low- and middle-income countries. Here is why they matter, and what it will take to lower their cost.
---

Remittances — the money migrants send back to their families — have long been underestimated as a force in global development finance. In 2023, officially recorded remittance flows to low- and middle-income countries reached over $650 billion, outpacing foreign direct investment (FDI) in many regions and far exceeding official development assistance (ODA).

## Why Remittances Matter

Unlike FDI or aid, remittances flow directly to households. They are countercyclical — they tend to increase during economic downturns in recipient countries, acting as a natural insurance mechanism for families and communities.

Key characteristics:

- **Resilience**: Remittances remained relatively stable even through the COVID-19 pandemic
- **Scale**: They represent 10% or more of GDP in over 30 countries
- **Reach**: Funds flow to rural and underserved communities that formal finance rarely reaches

## The Cost Problem

Despite their importance, the cost of sending remittances remains high. The global average cost of sending $200 is approximately 6.4%, more than three times the UN Sustainable Development Goal target of 3%. Sub-Saharan Africa faces the highest costs globally, averaging over 8%.

Reducing remittance costs by 5 percentage points would put an additional $32 billion per year directly in the hands of families in developing countries.

## The Path Forward

Reducing costs requires coordinated action across four areas:

1. **Regulatory reform** — Derisking policies that have led banks to exit remittance corridors must be reversed
2. **Digital infrastructure** — Expanding mobile money and digital payment rails in both sending and receiving countries
3. **Competition** — Encouraging new entrants including fintechs and mobile money operators to enter high-cost corridors
4. **Transparency** — Better data on pricing and flows to enable informed consumer choice and policy action
